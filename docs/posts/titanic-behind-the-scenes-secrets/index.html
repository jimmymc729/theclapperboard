Titanic (1997) Almost Sank Its Own Studio Before It Ever Hit Theaters — Here's What REALLY Happened On Set

Between the PCP-laced chowder, Kate Winslet nearly drowning, and a budget that made grown executives sweat, the making of James Cameron's Titanic was basically its own disaster movie.

1

Someone Spiked The Crew's Chowder With PCP And Sent Nearly 80 People To The ER

On what was supposed to be an ordinary night shooting the modern-day framing scenes in Nova Scotia, an unknown person dumped angel dust into the cast and crew's clam chowder. James Cameron and Bill Paxton both ate it, and the ensuing chaos — think conga lines, wheelchair wheelies, and a pen-stabbing incident — turned Dartmouth General Hospital into an absolute circus at 1am. To this day, nobody knows who did it.

2

Kate Winslet Nearly Drowned TWICE And Got Full-Blown Hypothermia

Filming the raft scene wasn't just emotionally devastating — it was physically brutal. Kate Winslet's coat once got caught on a submerged gate during a wave sequence, leaving her feeling like she'd burst before crew could free her, and she developed genuine hypothermia from refusing a wetsuit in freezing tank water that the production couldn't heat enough. She later told the LA Times she'd need serious money to work with Cameron again.

3

The Budget Doubled To $200 Million And Studio Execs Genuinely Panicked

Cameron originally pitched Titanic for around $110 million; by the time cameras stopped rolling, it had ballooned to roughly $200 million, making it the most expensive film ever produced at the time. Fox executives were so spooked they tried to sell off international rights and demanded an hour be cut from the runtime — Cameron's response was essentially 'fire me first,' and he ended up forfeiting his director's fee and profit share just to keep the studio calm.

4

'I'm The King Of The World!' Wasn't Even In The Script

That now-immortal line was invented on the spot when Cameron, stuck in a crane basket and losing daylight, couldn't get a scripted moment to land. He radioed Leonardo DiCaprio to just shout the line and sell it — DiCaprio's actual first reaction, more than once, was a confused 'What?' before he committed to the take that's now quoted in every Oscar speech parody imaginable.

5

The Grand Staircase Set Literally Ripped Itself Apart During Filming

When Cameron dumped 90,000 gallons of water onto the solid-oak Grand Staircase replica to simulate the ship's flooding, the force unexpectedly tore the entire structure off its steel-reinforced foundations. It was a one-take-only disaster since the set couldn't survive a second dunking, but Cameron later noted it likely mirrored exactly what happened to the real Titanic's staircase in 1912.

6

James Cameron Earned The Nickname 'The Scariest Man In Hollywood' On This Very Set

Between the megaphone screaming, the walkie-talkie tirades, and a reputation crew reportedly needed a backwards-spelled nickname ('Mij') just to vent about, Cameron's directing style on Titanic became the stuff of legend. Kate Winslet later admitted she was 'genuinely frightened' of him and that he had 'a temper like you wouldn't believe,' while co-star Danny Nucci simply called him 'a screamer.'
