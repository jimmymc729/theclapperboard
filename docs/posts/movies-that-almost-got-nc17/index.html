5 Movies That Went To War With The MPAA Just To Get An R Rating

One wrong cut and these films would've been branded with the box-office kiss of death — here's exactly what got trimmed, blurred, or fought over to squeak by.

1

Scorsese Had To Chop Down His Own Sex Scenes — Multiple Times

The Wolf of Wall Street was headed straight for an NC-17 over its wall-to-wall nudity and sex until Martin Scorsese agreed to trim things down. Scorsese told a reporter he was asked to cut more of the sex scenes but insisted nothing was forced out that made him uncomfortable, calling the process an accumulation of sexuality and drugs the board couldn't stomach at full volume.

2

Fox Quietly Trimmed Deadpool's Raunchiest Jokes To Keep It Playable

Deadpool leaned so hard into gross-out humor that even its already-R-rated cut had gags Fox wouldn't allow — director Tim Miller later admitted there was a bar-scene riff between Ryan Reynolds and T.J. Miller that got axed for being too vulgar for the film itself, on top of an entire deleted training montage full of masturbation and killing jokes. Miller has said one studio note that scared him wasn't even about content — it was Fox asking to make the fractured, fourth-wall-heavy story more linear, a request he pushed back on to protect the film's chaotic tone.

3

The Puppet Sex Scene That Took NINE Rewrites To Get Past Censors

Team America: World Police's marionette love scene was so explicit the MPAA made Trey Parker and Matt Stone submit nine separate versions before granting an R, ten days before release. Viacom's Robert Friedman said it came down entirely to "how often and how many positions," while Parker pointed out the same board barely blinked at puppets getting their heads blown off.

4

18 Seconds Stood Between American Psycho And Box-Office Death

Mary Harron's American Psycho was hit with an NC-17 over Patrick Bateman's threesome scene, and after the studio's appeal failed, just eighteen seconds were trimmed from the scene to flip it to an R. Lionsgate's Mark Urman argued the scene was "completely defensible within the context of the movie," but as he put it, "the MPAA didn't really get it" — even though the film's chainsaw murders sailed through untouched.

5

This Studio Said 'Screw It' And Released The NC-17 Cut Anyway

When the MPAA slapped Requiem for a Dream with an NC-17 over its climactic montage and denied Darren Aronofsky's appeal, Artisan Entertainment refused to cut a single frame and released it unrated instead. CEO Amir Malin stood firm, saying the company would "stand by director Darren Aronofsky's cut of the film and will not change a single frame of this important work" — a gamble that limited its theatrical reach but kept the movie intact.
