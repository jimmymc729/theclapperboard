Before She Broke The Multiverse, Michelle Yeoh Broke Bones (On Purpose, Kind Of)

From a teenage ballet injury to the first Best Actress Oscar ever won by an Asian woman, here's the wild real-life stunt reel behind the legend.

1

She Was Training To Be A Ballerina — Until Her Spine Had Other Plans

Michelle Yeoh started ballet at age four and studied at London's Royal Academy of Dance, dreaming of a professional dance career before a spinal injury shut that door. Her mother then entered her into a beauty pageant without asking — and Yeoh won Miss World Malaysia in 1983, going on to take Miss Moomba International in Australia the following year.

2

Then She Went And Became One Of Hong Kong's Most Fearless Stuntwomen

After a 1984 Jackie Chan commercial got her noticed, Yeoh signed with D&B Films and threw herself into the 'girls-with-guns' genre, doing her own fights and falls in movies like Yes, Madam! and Wing Chun. Director Stanley Tong later cast her opposite Chan in Police Story 3: Supercop, where she rode a motorcycle onto a moving train and leapt from a speeding truck onto his convertible — a sequence Quentin Tarantino called "the greatest stunts ever filmed in any movie ever."

3

She Actually Retired From Acting — Twice, Sort Of

In 1988 Yeoh married D&B Films executive Dickson Poon and stepped away from the business entirely, sitting out the industry for four years. After their 1992 divorce, she returned to the screen and was talked into Supercop, kicking off the second act of her Hong Kong action career.

4

She Made Bond History — Then Insisted On Throwing Her Own Punches

In 1997's Tomorrow Never Dies, Yeoh played Wai Lin, giving 007 a rare on-screen equal instead of a damsel to rescue. She came into the role as an already-established Hong Kong action star who did the majority of her own stunt work, and that reputation is exactly why the part was written to match her, not soften her.

5

For Crouching Tiger, She Took A Year Off Just To Train

Before shooting Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, Michelle Yeoh deliberately did not work for a year before filming began so she could concentrate on training and learning Mandarin. It didn't spare her from injury — she tore her ACL during an early fight scene and had to be flown to the U.S. for knee surgery mid-shoot.

6

It Took 95 Years Of Oscars History For This To Finally Happen

Despite four decades in the business, Everything Everywhere All At Once earned Yeoh her very first Oscar nomination — and she won, becoming the first Asian woman ever to take home Best Actress. She was also, at 60, the oldest nominee in her category that year, and used her speech to tell women everywhere they're never "past their prime."

7

The Deep Cuts: A Real-Life Biopic, A Starfleet Captaincy, And Producing Credits

Beyond the franchise hits, Yeoh played Aung San Suu Kyi in the 2011 biopic The Lady, and later became TV's Captain Philippa Georgiou on Star Trek: Discovery, the franchise's first Asian female Starfleet captain. She's also carried a producer credit for years, quietly shaping projects from behind the camera as well as in front of it.
